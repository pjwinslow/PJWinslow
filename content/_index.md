title: "Peter J. Winslow"
---

I am interested in understanding the limitations of proteins: exploring unexplored sequence space and probing how function emerges from chains of amino acids. The emergent properties of proteins are what make them so interesting. The number of possible sequences is so large that we will never see what every one of them does. That also means there is always some protein no one has ever seen, and it might do something that surprises us.

To pull the interesting sequences out of the noise, we have to understand the rules that govern this emergence of function. Life uses proteins for nearly every biochemical process. How and why did we arrive at the protein sequences we see today? Are amino acids the best chemistry for these tasks? If they are, would life elsewhere in the universe look the same? What are the true limits of proteins? These are the questions that the vast unexplored protein sequence space leads me to.

I am a fifth-year PhD candidate in the lab of Burckhard Seelig at the University of Minnesota, where I also completed my B.S. in Biochemistry. My work combines protein biochemistry, biophysics, structural biology, and protein language models, and is supported by a NASA FINESST grant.
